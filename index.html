Grade 9 at Colegio Colombo Americano

Part III

What we observed

How the issue was identified

It started with something we were experiencing ourselves.

As Grade 9 students, we first noticed academic pressure through our own experience. Conversations with classmates suggested it was not isolated, so we decided to investigate it systematically.

The order matters: the survey came after the observation, to test whether what we were noticing held beyond the five of us.

  1. 01Personal experienceOur own workload, our own weeks.
  2. 02ConversationsClassmates described the same thing.
  3. 03Formal surveySeven questions, 50 Grade 9 students.
  4. 04AnalysisPatterns, not confirmation.
  5. 05ActionBuilt around what the evidence showed.

Observation became evidence.

Fifty Grade 9 students answered. Everything that follows is built on what they said.

Part V

Connection to the course of action

Observed → measured → understood → decided

Before choosing an action we read the same evidence from each of these positions.

01

The student perspective

Academic pressure is the texture of an ordinary week: several summatives landing close together, instructions that are long before they are clear. Two thirds of our year group described that as constant or frequent.

Grounded inQ1 — 66% always or frequently stressed by homework or summatives. Q7 open responses describing how pending assignments feel.

02

The wellbeing perspective

The striking result is an absence: nobody selected “It doesn’t affect me”. Tiredness led, ahead of feeling overwhelmed and feeling anxious.

These are words students chose about themselves, not assessments. The data shows pressure being felt across the year group — not that any student has a diagnosed condition.

Grounded inQ3 — tired 23, overwhelmed 15, anxious 12, unaffected 0. Q2 — 70% report mood affected always or frequently.

03

The school-life perspective

Pressure does not stay inside the academic part of school: nearly three quarters said it reaches their relationships, at least sometimes. What looks like an individual workload issue also shapes the atmosphere of a year group.

Grounded inQ4 — 74% report an effect on relationships at school at least sometimes (37 of 50).

04

The support-structure perspective

Students are not routing pressure towards the adults whose role is to help: friends first, then silence, then parents. The teacher option was selected zero times out of fifty. That is a description of where a conversation currently goes, not a judgement of any teacher.

Example open responses

  • providing support with academic assingments
  • Yes, by taking me out of the classroom to talk about it
  • no

Reproduced exactly as written, spelling included, in answer to Q6. Microsoft Forms shows only the most recent responses, so these are illustrative extracts, not a coded analysis of all 50.

Grounded inQ5 — friends 25, keep it to myself 16, parents 9, teachers 0. Q6 — open responses.

05

The academic-organisation perspective

Workload is not only heavy; it is invisible until it arrives, and it clusters. A student rarely sees three summatives converging on the same two days until those days are here.

We cannot reduce the number of assessments or change the timetable. We can make workload visible earlier. Of everything the survey pointed to, this was the factor within a student team’s reach.

Grounded inQ1 and Q7 — stress attributed specifically to homework, summatives and pending assignments.

Part V · Decision

We decided to act

Choosing the course of action

The response was not another awareness poster. It was a tool students could actually use.

Awareness was not the missing piece — fifty students had just told us they were under pressure. So we worked back to the one factor we could move: how early workload becomes visible, and how a long instruction turns into a plan.

LeadTime AI helps students visualise workload, organise assignments, identify high-load days, break down long tasks and build a manageable work plan.

What this project does not claim

  • It does not treat, diagnose or cure anxiety, depression or any mental health condition.
  • It does not claim to solve student mental health, at this school or anywhere else.
  • It addresses academic organisation as one factor associated with student stress — the factor our evidence pointed to and our team could realistically affect.
  • It does not replace the school’s pastoral or professional support. Where a student needs that support, a planning tool is not a substitute for it.

Critical evaluation

The post-action survey suggests most respondents found the platform useful for the purpose it was designed for: organising academic work. It is self-reported perception, and does not demonstrate a reduction in stress or an improvement in mental health.

22% rated the platform as not very helpful or not helpful at all — the solution did not work equally well for every student.

User response · perceived usefulness

  • Positive3366%
  • Neutral612%
  • Negative1122%

These figures represent how useful students perceived the platform to be, not a measured change in their workload or wellbeing.

What worked

33 of 50 respondents — 66% — rated LeadTime AI as extremely or somewhat helpful for managing time and organising tasks.

This supports the team’s decision to focus the intervention on academic organisation.

What needs improvement

11 of 50 respondents — 22% — rated the platform as not very helpful or not helpful at all, while 12% were neutral.

The survey did not ask respondents to explain their rating, so we cannot identify specific causes from this evidence alone. Future feedback should ask which features were useful, difficult to use, or should be changed.

Part VIII

Reflection

A small reflection

What we learned

Academic pressure is not only about how hard individual tasks are — it is about how responsibilities accumulate. Research moved us from an assumption to evidence, and collaboration turned that evidence into a working outcome.

The post-action survey showed that most respondents found LeadTime AI useful, and also that one solution will not work equally for every student. If we continued, we would collect more qualitative feedback and refine the platform around what students actually say.
